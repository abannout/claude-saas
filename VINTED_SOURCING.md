# Vinted reselling in Germany: where to buy clothes (research, Sep 2026)

## 0. Read this first: rules and reality

- **Buying to resell makes you a business.**
  - Vinted requires business sellers to use **[Vinted Pro](https://www.vintagelab.org/blog/vinted-pro-deutschland-2026)** (free for the seller).
  - You need a **Gewerbe** registration (~€20–60 at your local Gewerbeamt).
  - Tip: the **Kleinunternehmerregelung** means no VAT up to €25k revenue in the previous year.
  - As a Pro seller you must offer the **14-day right of withdrawal** and show an **Impressum** (legal business details).
- **DAC7:** Vinted reports every seller with **≥30 sales or ≥€2,000 per year** to the tax office (BZSt) ([Vinted help](https://www.vinted.de/help/1149-dac-7-was-du-wissen-solltest)). Selling "privately" at volume won't stay hidden.
- **Counterfeits:** Vinted is full of fake branded items ([sellerforum.de](https://www.sellerforum.de/forum/thread/66701-klamotten-auf-vinted-oder-ebay-wo-verkauft-ihr-was/)). Selling fakes means a banned account plus trademark claims. Check labels and stitching, and buy only from suppliers who guarantee authenticity.
- **The maths:** at ~€10 profit per item, **€1,000–1,500 a month means 100–150 sales**, about 4–5 a day. Allow ~5–10 minutes per listing (photos + text) plus packing. Plan on **15–30 hours a month**.
- **Your profit is made when you buy** ([VintageLab](https://www.vintagelab.org/blog/vinted-trends-juni-2026)). A cheap kilo that doesn't sell loses more money than an expensive kilo that sells within days.

## 1. Sources, from cheapest (most work) to most expensive (least work)

### Tier 1: Local and private sources (highest margin, most time)
| Source | How | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Flea markets** (Flohmärkte) | Go at opening time, pay cash, haggle | Best margin; find dates on your city's website or on marktcom.de |

## 2. What sells on Vinted (focus your sourcing)
- **Branded sportswear and streetwear:** vintage Nike and Adidas, Carhartt, Ralph Lauren, Levi's (e.g., 501), Tommy Hilfiger.
- **Outerwear:** The North Face, Patagonia, Barbour. **Buy out of season** (winter jackets in summer) and sell in autumn/winter ([VintageLab](https://www.vintagelab.org/blog/vinted-trends-juni-2026)).
- **Trends:** Y2K, 90s sportswear, American college sweatshirts.
- **Football shirts:** current national team shirts during tournaments, **original shirts from old World Cup years** sell at good margins.
- **Kids' clothing bundles:** steady demand from parents.
- **Avoid:** cheap fast fashion (H&M, Primark, Shein basics). It sells slowly and for very little.

---

## 3. Vetting a supplier (before you pay)
1. **Order a sample box first** (10–25 kg), never a full bale.
2. Ask for **grade definitions** (A/B/C) and **photos or video of the actual bale or box**.
3. Check reviews (Trustpilot, Google, Reddit, Vinted reseller groups) and whether the supplier has an **Impressum and a registered company**.
4. **Avoid bank-transfer prepayment to unknown sellers.** Scams are common. Use PayPal Goods & Services or pay by card.
5. Get a **proper invoice** for your business costs.
6. Ask for an **authenticity guarantee** on branded items.

---

## 4. Starter plan (budget €300–500)
1. **Weeks 1–2:** register a Gewerbe and switch to Vinted Pro. Buy €100–150 from Tier 1 (flea market + 3–5 Kleinanzeigen bundles).
2. **Weeks 2–3:** buy **one curated Grade-A test box** (Tier 3, ~€150–250).
3. **Weeks 3–6:** list everything with consistent photos (plain background, front/back/label/flaws) and fair prices. Bundle discounts clear slow stock.
4. **Track per source:** cost per piece, sell-through after 30 days, profit per hour.
5. **Scale only the winning source.** Add Tier 4/5 only once you're selling 100+ items a month.
